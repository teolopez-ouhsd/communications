Typography

Two free fonts, one job each. Montserrat for headlines, Open Sans for everything you read.

2 font families · free on Google FontsUpdated October 7, 2026

Primary typeface: Montserrat

Required
Back to School Night

Headlines, titles, callouts, and section headers. Use SemiBold (600), Bold (700), or ExtraBold (800). Avoid thin and light weights.

Get Montserrat (opens Google Fonts in a new tab)

Secondary typeface: Open Sans

Required
Every student, every day. Paragraphs, captions, forms, and web text read best in Open Sans Regular.

Use Regular (400) for body, SemiBold (600) for labels and buttons, and Italic for light emphasis.

Get Open Sans (opens Google Fonts in a new tab)

Display and headline type

Recommended

For big moments like banners and title slides, use Montserrat ExtraBold in all caps with a little extra letter spacing. Keep it to a few words.

Class of 2027

Schools may have their own display fonts, like Pacifica's Triton Block. Use those only for that school's materials.

Web-safe alternatives

Recommended

Only when you truly can't install or select the brand fonts, like in some email tools or older systems.

Good news: Google Docs, Google Slides, and Canva include both brand fonts. Pick them from the font menu.

Licensing and source

Required

Both fonts are free for any use, including print, web, and commercial work, under the SIL Open Font License. Download them only from Google Fonts. School fonts, like Triton Block, are for that school's materials only. Download them from the school's Brand Center.
