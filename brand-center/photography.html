Photography

Real students, real moments, real light. Our photos should show what it feels like to be here.

Permissions first

Required

Before you post a student's photo, check that the family hasn't opted out of media release. Your school office can tell you who is on the opt-out list.

  • Never post a student's full name with a photo without permission.
  • Never show student IDs, schedules, home addresses, or screens with student data.
  • If someone asks you not to take or post their photo, respect it.

Photography philosophy

Recommended

Authentic

Candid moments over posed lineups. Show learning as it happens.

Inclusive

Over time, show every school, program, and kind of student.

Hopeful

Bright, natural light and genuine expressions.

Student-centered imagery

Recommended
  • Students are the subject. Adults support the story.
  • Get to eye level. Kneel for seated students.
  • Faces and hands at work tell more than wide shots of rooms.
  • Stock photos are a last resort. Never use stock photos to represent our own students.

Classroom photography

Recommended
  • Ask the teacher first and keep it quiet. Shoot during activity, not lecture.
  • Look for hands-on moments: labs, shops, art, collaboration.
  • Check backgrounds for student data on screens and whiteboards.

Athletics

Recommended
  • Capture peak action: the catch, the finish line, the celebration.
  • Feature our athletes. Opponents can appear, but our team leads the frame.
  • Include the fans, band, and spirit squads. They're part of the story.
  • No flash during play.

Events

Recommended
  • Shot list: wide room shot, speakers, audience reactions, details, and group photos.
  • Graduation: name walk, diploma handoff, cap toss, families in the stands.
  • Arrive early for setup and empty-venue shots.

Portraits and headshots

Recommended
  • Plain, uncluttered background. Soft, even light on the face.
  • Frame from mid-chest up with a little space above the head.
  • Staff headshots: same background and crop for everyone on a page.

Community imagery

Recommended

Show students connected to Oxnard, Port Hueneme, Camarillo, and the surrounding area: internships, service projects, local partners, and the places families know.

Composition

Recommended
Rule of thirdsPlace the subject on a grid line, not dead center.
Look into the frameLeave space in the direction the subject faces or moves.
Get closerFill the frame. Step in before you zoom.

Cropping

Recommended
  • Don't crop at joints like wrists, elbows, knees, or ankles.
  • Keep a little room above heads.
  • Crop for each platform: 4:5 for Instagram feed, 9:16 for stories, 16:9 for web banners. See graphic sizes.

Color treatment

Recommended
  • Natural, true-to-life color. Correct white balance so skin tones look real.
  • No heavy filters, color tints, or vintage effects.
  • Never recolor uniforms or school colors.

Recommended and discouraged

Recommended

Recommended

  • Students actively learning, building, performing, competing
  • Natural light, candid expressions
  • Diverse groups across programs and schools
  • Clear focus on one subject

Discouraged

  • Backs of heads, empty rooms, blurry crowds
  • Students on the media opt-out list
  • Heavy filters, stretched or pixelated images
  • Generic stock photos as our students, or AI-generated people
