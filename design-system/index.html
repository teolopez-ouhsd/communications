One district. One look. Built once, used everywhere.

A design system is a shared kit of ready-made parts and written rules, so every tool the district builds looks and works like it came from the same place. This page explains what that means and how your team starts in about ten minutes.

What a design system is

Think of a classroom setup. A teacher can walk into any room at any school and know where the board, the outlets, and the exits are. Nobody redesigned the room for that teacher, and no teacher had to figure it out alone.

A design system does the same for software. Instead of every team choosing its own colors, buttons, and form layouts, the district provides one set that is already designed, tested for accessibility, and approved. Your team builds the part that is unique to your app, and the system handles how it looks and behaves.

Why it matters

Four reasons, and the first one is for families.

Trust

A parent who sees the same look and the same behavior in every district tool knows it is real. A different look every time looks like a scam.

Speed

Buttons, forms, tables, and pages already exist. Teams skip hundreds of small decisions and ship sooner.

Accessibility and compliance

The district must meet WCAG 2.1 AA. Every part in the system is built and checked for it, so each team does not carry that risk alone.

Consistency with AI

AI assistants will happily invent a new style every time. A written system, with rules the AI can read, keeps what they build on brand.

Start in ten minutes

You do not need to be a designer. You need these four steps.

  1. Download the rules for AI

    Save DESIGN.md into the root folder of your project.

  2. Pick the closest template

    Open the sign in, dashboard, form, or list and detail template, and save it as your starting page.

  3. Give your AI assistant this prompt

    Replace the bracket with what you are building.

    Read DESIGN.md in this project and follow it exactly. Build [describe your app and who uses it]. Start from the closest template in design-system/templates. Use only OUHSD design system classes and tokens, and never invent colors, fonts, or sizes. When you finish, run the self-check at the end of DESIGN.md and tell me anything the system could not cover.

  4. Check before you launch

    Run through the ready to ship checklist. It takes five minutes and covers keyboard use, phone width, and Spanish.

What we ask of every team

Three commitments keep the system working for everyone.

One

Link, do not copy

Use the published stylesheet at a versioned address, so fixes and improvements reach you safely.

Two

Use the parts, do not invent

Use the provided classes and tokens. No custom colors, fonts, or sizes, including when AI suggests them.

Three

Ask when something is missing

If you need a part that does not exist, ask Communications and Marketing. A good request becomes a component for every team.

Common questions

Do we have to use it?
Yes, for any web app that families, students, or staff use on behalf of the district. This is how the district stays consistent and accessible. Internal experiments and prototypes are welcome to start from it too.
What if our app needs something the system does not have?
Use the closest existing component for now and ask Communications and Marketing. Please do not build a one-off. If the need is real, it joins the next version and every team benefits.
Will an update break our live app?
No. Your app links to a versioned address (v1). Small fixes arrive automatically. Anything that changes how existing markup looks is published as a new version (v2), and you move when you are ready.
Can a school have its own colors?
Yes, through a school theme. A theme swaps only the school accent: the header stripe, accent cards, and school badges. Action red, links, focus rings, and status colors stay the same everywhere so every app behaves alike. Themes exist for Pacifica, Del Sol, and Channel Islands, with a template for the rest.
Does it work with Wix, Google Sites, or Apps Script?
It works anywhere you can add a stylesheet link or paste a style block, including Google Apps Script web apps and custom HTML embeds. Platforms that block external CSS can paste the contents of the stylesheet instead.
Does the system replace accessibility testing?
No. It removes the hardest problems, but your content and layout choices still matter. Every app still needs the keyboard, zoom, and screen reader checks in the ready to ship checklist.
